# The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (`0400486024DEC000`)

A Switch 2 game: update 1.1.1 (v131072), SDK 22.2.8 (`Ounce`). Nintendo EPD's engine (sead, agl, gsys, Havok, CRIWARE; project `Owl`, with Tears of the Kingdom's file layout) on NVN2. Modules `main`, `subsdk0` (NintendoSDK_movie) and `sdk`; the rewriter's patches are a frame-step stub and one cut ([`patches.py`](patches.py)). Status: plays on the Mac through the title, the opening, a new file's first scene, Link's house and Kokiri Forest, with saves, at about 59 fps with the picture close to console video.

### Screen shape (`DM_SCREEN_ASPECT`, current)

On v131072, `DM_SCREEN_ASPECT` (1.3 to 1.77, e.g. `1.6` for 16:10 or `1.333` for 4:3) runs nineteen hooks installed together under `MATCH_FRAME_STEP`. Every required function must match, including the pane array allocator and constructor, before the rewriter writes the `SCREEN_ASPECT` capability that exposes *Picture shape*. The dynamic-resolution match is independent. At state `+0x48` of zero the hooks leave the original values in place. The aspect hooks cover eleven height sites across `graphics init`, `scene app init`, `base scene defaults`, `display mgr defaults`, `base res read`, and `display target a/b/c`, `perspective proj` (`sead::PerspectiveProjection::doUpdateMatrixImpl_` at `0x3485cc0`), devirtualized inlined projection updates `camera update` (`main+0x33b82c`) and `device proj` (`main+0x23b9a8`), the `Layout2` ortho builders `layout ortho` (`main+0x1520c80`) and `layout view ortho` (`main+0x773b90`), `layout calc matrix` (`main+0x14e9980`), and `pane global matrix` (`main+0x152caa0`, the edge hook below), and the movie viewport. The `Layout2` ortho stubs guard on `oh != 0`, `width >= 1000.0`, and `|width*9 - height*16| < 4.0`, scaling height by `state +0x5c` (`(16/9) / ratio`) so capture textures keep their native sizes and 2D HUD elements keep a 1:1 pixel ratio inside the centred 16:9 band while 3D rendering expands Vert+ (preserving 16:9 horizontal framing with extra vertical view); `layout calc matrix` scales full-screen PicturePanes (`width >= 1000`, `height >= 700`, `|width*9 - height*16| < 200`) so scene faders and wipes cover the expanded canvas without un-faded bands at top and bottom.

The HUD and the menu bars at the screen's edges ([`shape_anchor.c`](shape_anchor.c), `_shape_anchor` in `patches.py`): the ortho stubs leave every layout in the centred 16:9 band, so `pane global matrix` calls `shape_calc` at `main+0x152ce50`, after a pane's global matrix is computed and before it is stored, for panes with a parent and root panes alike. In the HUD layouts (hearts, buttons, minimap, action guides, timers, location names) the first pane of a chain whose translation is past the 16:9 edge region (`|y| >= 650` of 2160) moves out by the extra half-height; in the menu frames `MenuOverlay_00` (top bar node 5, guide row 31) and `SystemOverlay_00` (title bar 2 and 4, bottom bar 3 and 7) those nodes move with their children. Indexed edits check the pane array bounds, the authored name, authored and live class, and any authored dimensions the edit uses. `MapMenu_00` holds both the minimap and the Minus map page: the page's subtrees (nodes 16, 81, 90, 95) count as menu, and the two layers the game places at the minimap, at (1490, -644), just inside the edge region (node 64, and node 82, the icon layer under node 81 that carries the player arrow and the exits), move down with the minimap when they sit there (x > 1000); on the map page the game puts node 82 at the centre and it stays. A child's matrix is computed from its parent's stored one, so each widget moves whole, with its labels, counts and icons; lists, centred dialogs and the map page stay in the band. The chain's state rides in the translation's z as a mark (`-3/1024` menu, `-4/1024` HUD, `-5/1024` moved), which the UI projection leaves invisible. At 16:9 the trampoline bypasses the helper and its stack traffic.

Menu backgrounds that end at the 16:9 band (`pane_edit` in `shape_anchor.c`): no game art is stretched, scaled or cut. Art keeps its 16:9 size or moves; only plain shading is made larger. `PlusMenu_00`: the pause pages' side gradients (2, 3, 5, 6) reach the real top and bottom, each page's bottom gradient (11 Collection, 12 Equipment, 13 Items, the page art's own shade, even sideways) moves to the real bottom at its own height and widens to the screen's width about its page's centre (the pages are 4280 apart, so two never overlap while they slide), and the shade under the top bar (20) moves up with the bar. `SystemLoadList_00`: the list's clipping window (5) grows by the extra height at its bottom, so the list starts where it does at 16:9 and shows more rows; its rows, which the game lays from the window's top edge, are put back (marks `-6/1024` window, `-7/1024` row). `MapMenu_00`: the map page's dark backdrops (11, 17, 95) fill the screen's height, the parchment keeps its 16:9 size, and the world map's soft side shades (93, 94) reach the edges. `ChallengeMenu_01` (Threads of Time): the tapestry mask stays at its authored size. Its picture wrapper is 0x60 bytes (`main+0x1529f4c`), and the reviewed asset has no texture-transform block in its picture definition. The PR’s earlier write at wrapper `+0x100` fell outside the object. Key Items and the Minus map's parchment keep their 16:9 size.

The movies (`movie draw`, `main+0x3b5c710`: the title screen's background and the opening): the draw's viewport is the whole frame buffer, so the 16:9 movie filled a taller frame, 1.33x taller at 4:3 (the title's moon an oval). At `+0x64`, after the viewport is built and before `main+0x2846e0` applies it (viewport and scissor), a rect taller than 16:9 becomes 16:9 about its centre, with black above and below; the art keeps its shape as the menu backgrounds do. The title's moon, height / width (3 frames each): 4:3 1.27 to 1.33 before, 0.98 to 1.01 after; 16:10 1.09 to 1.12 before, 0.99 to 1.01 after; 3:2 0.99 to 1.01 after; 16:9 0.99 to 1.01.

**3D view.** On a taller screen, the three projection stubs (`perspective proj`, `camera update`, `device proj`) update `sead::PerspectiveProjection` in place for ~16:9 projections (`|aspect - 16/9| <= 0.01`) before reloading `tan` and `aspect` to build the matrix: `+0xb0` (`aspect`) takes the screen ratio `r` (`state +0x58`), and when Vert+ is active (`state +0x60 > 0`, the default; `DM_SCREEN_FOV=off` sets `-1.0` for Hor-) `+0xac` (`tan`), `+0xa4`/`+0xa8` (`sin`/`cos`), and `+0xa0` (`fovy = 2 * atan(tan * k)`) are updated too, with a 64-entry ring of widened `fovy` bit patterns (`state +0x2fc/+0x300..+0x3fc`) guarding against double-widening if a widened `fovy` is copied into another 16:9 projection. Before this fix, `47578ded` changed only the matrix-builder registers while leaving the stored object at 16:9, so `camera update` (`main+0x33ab44`/`0x33ab64`) and the scene view (`main+0x235be0`, `tanf` at `main+0x237468`) culled and fitted sun shadows with the 16:9 frustum: objects popped at the top and bottom edges and the ground turned dark in shadow past a line ([issue #3202](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/issues/3202), [PR #3209](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/pull/3209)). Verified headless on `Mac18,5` (macOS 27.2.1, Xcode 27.2, cached chain `13b3bdb97e7e`, Kakariko Village default, pitched-up and pitched-down views, 2026-11-23): pre-fix `47578ded` at 4:3 reproduced both the bottom-edge culling/dark band and the pitched-down dark shadow line across the steps; after the fix, `16:10` (`2560x1600`) and `4:3` (`2560x1920`) both preserved exact 16:9 horizontal framing with extra vertical view and clean shadows/culling at all edges, `DM_SCREEN_FOV=off` at 16:10 showed Hor- framing, and `16:9` was unchanged. Physical iPad and iPhone Duo verification remain pending.

Verified on the Mac (v131072, Kokiri/Goron City gameplay): `16:9` (`0`, `2560x1440`), `16:10` (`1.6`, `2560x1600`), and `4:3` (`1.333`, `2560x1920`) all keep `heart1` at `68x33` (`h/w = 0.485`, shifted vertically by half the extra height) with unstretched HUD widgets and Vert+ 3D framing.

The revised hooks matched all 26 indexed pane rules against the v131072 layout archive. A headless run on an M4 Pro (macOS 27.2.1, Xcode 27.2, cached chain `df1800562702`) presented `2560x1920` at 4:3. The captured title movie was `2560x1440` with 240 black rows above and below. The Items pause menu showed the top bar and guide row at the edges with a round Bomb preview. Physical iPad verification of the revised title movie is pending.

## Dynamic resolution

- The controller (v131072; found by fingerprint, so the addresses are only for reading): the parameters are `game__gfx__DynamicResolutionParam` (`LoadThreshold`, `ReduceCounterMax`, `IncreaseCounterMax`, and a list of levels, each a `ResolutionReduceScale` and a `Priority`). Once a frame the update (`main+0x805aa0`) reads the frame's GPU time, subtracts what its cost model predicts at the current size and keeps the trend of that residual. The level function (`main+0x8065e0`) adds the residual to the model's cost at each level and returns the best level under the threshold: 0, the full 1536x864, when the current size fits. The update moves down after `ReduceCounterMax` frames that ask for it and back up after `IncreaseCounterMax`. Scripted `GPULoad` hints from event data (`main+0x31c3de0`) and `OneshotSetDynamicResolutionLoadThreshold` adjust it in cutscenes.
- Resolved on the host: after a loading screen it sat at its floor, 683x384, for minutes while the frame held 60 fps with GPU time to spare. A stale frame-start timestamp and event waits that idled the GPU made its timer read more than the frame took: [Dynamic resolution at its floor while the GPU has time to spare](../../../docs/reference/frame-rate.md#dynamic-resolution-at-its-floor-while-the-gpu-has-time-to-spare).
- Resolved in the game's model: with the host's timer right it still sat at 683x384 for good. The update takes the frame's load (about 0.2 ms a unit) less its small fixed-cost model, divides the rest by the square of the current scale, and the level function multiplies that back by each level's scale squared. The whole remaining cost is taken to grow with the pixel count, as on the Switch 2 its constants were tuned for. Here it hardly does: 683x384 cost 14.3 ms and 1536x864 14.7 ms on an M4 Pro (mesh, compute and the upscaler's fixed 2048x1152 dominate). A probe of the controller in play (levels 0..9 at 0.75..0.333 of 2048x1152, threshold 83, about 16.4 ms) read a load of 70-80 at 683x384, under the threshold. The extrapolation still put full size at 300-400, so it never stepped up.
- Current: the launcher's **Dynamic resolution** (`DM_DYNAMIC_RESOLUTION`, off by default). Off holds full resolution. On climbs one level at a time on the load measured at each level, with no model of how the cost grows, within the game's own level list, threshold and counters:
  - the threshold is the game's 83 (about 16.4 ms) at 60 fps. ### Game data

The field names, file layout and memory offsets below were checked on v131072 (`main` build ID `e0f3ccfaf50065ae4ea01eb5aeb13b1a`) on a 16 GB M4 MacBook Pro with macOS 26.7. [PR #1961](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/pull/1961)

- **Field names.** RomFS holds `GameData/GameDataList.Product.110.byml.zs`, a big-endian BYML v7 (`BY`) compressed with the zstd dictionary `zs.zsdic` from the SARC `Pack/ZsDic.pack.zs`. Entries store the MurmurHash3 hash of the full name with seed 0. Each struct lists member-name hashes alongside full-name hashes. Hashing strings from `main` and the decompressed `AI`, `Logic`, `Sequence`, `Event`, `UI`, `Game`, `GameParameter`, `Component` and `Pack/Actor` files identified 4,595 entries. Following the struct members brought the count to 7,136. Enum values also store the hash of their names, for example `Fairy`.
- **Player progress.** `PlayerStatus.Life` and `PlayerStatus.MaxLife` are in quarter hearts (80 is 20 hearts). `PlayerStatus.MP` and `PlayerStatus.MPMax` use 240 units per meter. Other fields include `PlayerStatus.CurrentRupee`, equipped gear (`EquipWeapon`, `EquipShield`, `EquipArmor`, `EquipBoot`), and `AssignableItem.BottleInfo.Bottle[A-E].IsValid` and `.BottleItem` for bottle ownership and contents. Item counts use `PouchData.<actor>.StockNum` with `IsGet.<actor>` for ownership. Songs use `IsGet.Item_Melody_<song>`, for example `IsGet.Item_Melody_SariaSong`. Dungeon fields use `DungeonInfo.<dungeon>.SmallKeyNum`, `.IsGetMap`, `.IsGetCompass` and `.IsGetBossKey`, for example under `ForestTemple`.
- **Capacities.** `GameParameter/ExpansionMaxNumActor/<bag>` in the bag's actor pack gives capacities of 20 / 30 / 40 for bombs and bombchus, 30 / 40 / 50 for arrows and Deku seeds, 20 / 30 / 40 for Deku nuts, and 10 / 20 / 30 for Deku sticks. The medium and large wallets hold 500 and 1,000 rupees.
- **Save image in memory.** After the header, `progress.sav` holds hash and u32 value pairs for booleans, integers, floats and enums, followed by strings and vectors. The in-memory address of a field was `heap + 0x5A4DA5D8 + <file offset>` in six save slots across two launches. Changes to item counts, ownership flags, rupees, ammo, dungeon items and bottle contents remained in memory through pausing and walking. Farore's Wind appeared in play after writing its count and ownership flag. Other menu screens remain unchecked.
- **Live health and magic.** The game read `Life` and `MP` from the save image on area load. In play, the reporter found four consecutive integers (`Life`, `MaxLife`, `MP`, `MPMax`) at `heap + 0x1585A3508` in the Great Fairy's Fountain and Zora's Domain, with HUD copies near `heap + 0x59F1B394` and `heap + 0x71FB05AC`. The live block's location in other areas and the HUD refresh path remain open.

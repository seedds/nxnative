# Pokémon Pokopia (`0400A3A01EE4C000`)

A Switch 2 game: update v655360 (the title screen says Ver. 2.0.1), SDK 20.5.25 built for the Switch 2 (`Ounce`). Koei Tecmo's KTGL engine on NVN2, modules `main`, `subsdk0` (WebM movies) and `sdk`. **Plays on the Mac**: the opening, the character choice and the first area's tutorial at about 60 fps, including 31 minutes of the user's playtest with no GPU error and no crash. A save from an earlier run loads and continues. No IPA has been built.

| Setting | Where | Why |

## Files

- A dump folder whose `updates/<version>/` holds the whole RomFS; [`pack_dump.py`](../../../scripts/pack_dump.py) packs it without a base RomFS. RomFS: the newer RDB (as ROMANCE OF THE THREE KINGDOMS 8 REMAKE's) under `motor_asset_re/KIDS_PLATFORM_OUNCE`, and a `LD_C0_R.BIN` / `LI_C0_R.IDX` LINKDATA pair.
- Shaders: 131,426 in [`shader_map.tsv`](shader_map.tsv) ([KTGL on NVN2](../../../docs/reference/shaders.md#ktgl-on-nvn2-switch-2-glslc-outputs-and-ld_--li_-linkdata)). The Mac playtest's library held only the 8,157 that its runs asked for (with their pixel shaders), listed in [`shader_seen.tsv`](shader_seen.tsv); the NSP script's `--seen-shaders` builds those alone ([Building selected Mac shaders](../../../docs/development.md#building-selected-mac-shaders)). Such a library has no stamp and no `c1_full.tsv`, and each start logs that in red. On chain `20b9b1c2e4e4` an M4 Pro with other jobs running took 18 minutes for the 8,158 shaders of the list and the last logs (Metal's compile cache held nearly all of its libraries: 113 compiled), or 16 minutes with all of them cached, then about 5 minutes for the 7.9 GB Mac app. That app boots to walking at about 16.7 ms a frame with no GPU error and no shader reported missing, and later areas are untested on it. The NSP script builds all 131,426: an estimated 2.5 hours on an M4 Pro (from 1,001 shaders; no full build has run) since [the builder stopped keeping the 4.4 GB of packages in memory](../../../docs/reference/shaders.md#a-shader-build-that-keeps-its-packages-in-memory-runs-one-worker-at-a-time) and [its compiles stopped instantiating Metal's texture classes again](../../../docs/reference/shaders.md#each-metal-compile-instantiates-metals-texture-classes-again) and [its Python stopped limiting it](../../../docs/reference/shaders.md#rewrites-that-try-every-character-of-a-large-source) (before all of these, players saw about one shader a second and ETAs of 20 to 200 hours: GitHub issue #1036). The map's 105,534 pixel shaders each have `_ba` and `_bl` libraries, 57% of its compiles before those whose MSL matches another's, while the runs on the 8,157-shader library opened none of them (file access times; 7,985 of its 8,157 `_b` libraries were opened). Those runs only reached the first area (open).
- No `pso_manifest.tsv` yet, so each start also logs a red line for the missing seed list. In the runs here no draw waited on it.

## Shaders

- Resolved: `cc679c7b4d3e703c` did not translate (`LDC with a bindless bank or an address mode`): an indexed read of c1 with the bank in the address register, found on Hyrule Warriors: Age of Imprisonment, which ships the same shader ([LDC with the bank in its address register](../../../docs/reference/shaders.md#ldc-with-the-bank-in-its-address-register-is)); all 8,158 shaders of the playtest library now translate, and the walk below logged no shader failing to load. The census also lists f55 three times, two more of that LDC and 16 BRX with no target: whether they are the forms since translated ([Barrier moves around warp shuffles](../../../docs/reference/shaders.md#barrier-moves-around-warp-shuffles)) is not checked (open).
- A `3D binding fallback` line fires once at about frame 2100: a 2D 8x8 texture in a 3D slot. The shader is not identified (open).

## Rendering

- Frames take about 16.7 ms. A cold file cache, as after a reboot, keeps the game on the loading icon for over a minute, and a clean run there says nothing: check that `[nvn] frame N: … calls` reached about 3,500.
- The lit HDR target is RG11B10, exposure a 2x1 RGBA32F image, tone map `075fd7285c64b798` with a 32³ LUT, final `ed5f4c6386ca621d`, the luminance histogram compute `2118ab534fac88c6`.
- `texraw` pads rows: an RG11B10 dump has a 16-byte stride with 4-byte pixels at the head of each row. It returns one slice of a 3D texture, and serials shift between runs.

## Screen shape wider than 16:9 (`DM_SCREEN_ASPECT`, current)

On the v327680 build (main `816c5358…`; the stubs are in [`patches.py`](patches.py), `screen_aspect wide` in [`game.tsv`](game.tsv)), `DM_SCREEN_ASPECT=2.37` (launcher setting *Picture shape*: 21:9 2560x1080, 21:9 3440x1440, 32:9) renders a picture of the screen's shape, 1080 tall: the host writes the output width (state +0xa0, a multiple of 8) and its ratio (+0xa4) and presents that shape (`loader.c`). KTGL takes its sizes from four places, and each widens a 16:9 size by the output width / 1920:

- **Render targets**: every one is made from a descriptor at `main+0x16ae780` (the scene's 1920x1080 targets and their 1/2 .. 1/24 chains), except those the album makes (`main+0x100dea0`: two 1920x1080, 25 480x270 photo thumbnails, two 320x180, ten 640x360), which the saved photos fill as pixels of those widths. Widened, the title screen's two photos came out sheared into repeated strips (the user's playtest); state +0xac is set from that function's start to its end and the stub leaves sizes alone while it is set.
- **The swap chain**: its three textures come from `main+0x105c0bc` (descriptor +0x14 / +0x18).
- **The render width**: `main+0x103a1d0` reads the dynamic resolution table `main+0x2756530` (34 widths 960 .. 1920 in steps of 32, then 2560, 3840, 7680; heights at `main+0x27565b8`), `main+0x103a1c0` and the table's out-of-range case give 1920. The frame's viewports and the window crop follow it. With the targets alone, the scene filled 1920 of the 2560 and the rest showed other passes' contents (the frame's G-buffer, lighting and composite were drawn 1920 wide, at 1728x972 when the dynamic resolution stood at 0.9).
- **The renderer's own 1920.0**: the ground's depth and G-buffer passes take their viewport as the dynamic resolution scale x 1920.0 x 1080.0 (`main+0x104f364`, `main+0x104f61c`; at 0.9, 1728x972 where every other pass had 2304x972, so outdoors the terrain stopped at the 16:9 edge and was drawn narrower), as do `main+0xfff1c4` and `main+0xfff450`; `main+0xd3aac` and `main+0x100edd4` write 1920.0 x 1080.0 into a pass's constants, `main+0x1018bac` / `main+0x1018c08` an object's screen size. Each takes the output width as a float (state +0xa8). Found by watching the viewport the context records (`main+0x16d0b00`, writer `main+0x16d0b40`) for the 1728 value; the `mov wN, #0x44f00000` form (one instruction, no `movk`) had escaped a search for 1920.0 built in two. The HUD code's own 1920.0 (its scale, min(width / 1920, height / 1080), 1 at 1080 tall) stays.
- **Full-screen pictures**: at the same place, a picture or window pane (type +0x112 1 or 3) at least 1920 wide whose parent is a null pane widens its x axis by the output width / 1920. Without it the title screen and the loading screen kept black bars, and the fades (title to loading, loading to the world, waking up) covered only the middle 16:9 band while the world showed at the sides (the user's playtest on a 2560x1080 screen). The title screen's art stretches with it; the loading screen's Ditto and caption are panes of their own and keep their shape.
- **The gameplay HUD**: every layout is a 1920x1080 canvas centred on the screen, its panes placed from the centre (anchor byte +0x10d is 0x11 on every pane seen), so a wider screen alone leaves the HUD in the middle 16:9 band. Where `main+0x1e822a0` stores a pane's global matrix (`main+0x1e82690`), the panes of the gameplay HUD layout that stand in the edge band, listed by name hash (the button guide, the tool wheel and seven more of that layout), move out by the extra half-width, their children with them. The same rule on every layout's edge-band panes (direct children of a 1920x1080 canvas, at most 640 wide) moved the HUD too but pulled the main menu's pieces apart (its coin counter and status pills moved, its device art did not), so the rule names the HUD's panes.
- **The cameras**: `main+0x1e225d4` builds a camera's projection from +0x148 fovy and +0x14c aspect (+0x140 near, +0x144 far, setter `main+0x1e22828`). All five 3D cameras of the first area have exactly 16/9, so a camera of 16/9 takes the screen's ratio (Hor+: the vertical angle stays, the sides widen), stored back so that culling and shadows read it too.
- **The UI's text and map scale**: KTGL's text and the map beside the menu take a screen scale, the screen's width / 1920 (`main+0xd3ca0` returns the screen object's width, `+0x48`), which assumes a 16:9 screen like the canvas. At 2560 it came out 1.33 while the canvas stays 1920 wide: the letter spacing (FontSpace, which `main+0x9c15c8` stores at text `+0x1f4` times that scale: -5 became -6.67) and the glyphs were squeezed while the measured texts grew, so every button bar's pills widened and their texts slid to the right of them (the bag's Info / Drop / Hold to Sort bar, Save & Quit, Put Away / Eat), and the map's icons spread out from its centre (the user's playtest). Found by diffing every pane between 16:9 and 21:9 (only the text's `+0x1f4` differed) and a write watch on it (`DM_PROBE_WATCH=heap+…`). The 37 calls of `main+0xd3ca0` that make that scale take the canvas's width instead, min(width, height x 16 / 9) (the same on a 16:9 or taller screen); two keep the screen's: `main+0x100c8ec`, which builds the UI's mapping onto the whole screen (2 / width), and `main+0x101f88c`, a GPU viewport of the screen's width times a render scale. Screen-space rects (`main+0xdea5xx`: a size at `+0x18` and a centre at `+0x48` in the canvas, scaled into pixels by the call at `main+0xdea66c`) were then placed from the screen's left edge: the door wipe (a house-shaped mask around the player and four full-canvas black fills) stopped 1920 pixels in, so the outside showed on the right while the left was black and the house sat left of the player (the user's playtest), and the map's icons (placed in the canvas's top-left coordinates by `main+0x9f52b0`) landed (width - 1920) / 2 to the left. A rect's centre x moves by width / 2 - 960 x scale (`rect_x` stub) and a rect of the whole canvas spans the whole width (`rect_w`), so the house stays unstretched and centred with black across the screen (resolved; an earlier `icons` stub that moved only the map's icons was folded into it). A point in the world onto the canvas (`main+0x100c60c`, the anchors of in-world prompts and speech bubbles) mapped clip space to 0 .. 1920, which spans the screen only at 16:9: the canvas x is x_ndc x width / 2 + 960 (`anchor` stub; a Pokémon's "Hello, hello!" at the screen's right edge stands above it). Checked headless at 21:9 against 16:9 on the bag, the menu, the Pokédex pages, the outdoor map, the button guide and the pick-up prompt: the same sizes and places; at 16:9 nothing moved.

Checked on the Mac (headless, the user's save, 2560x1080): the hut, the walk out of it to the first outdoor area (the ground to both edges, the HUD's Nearby Habitats panel, button guide and tool wheel at the screen's edges), the main menu and the bag; no red line. The same route at 16:9 with the patched build looks as before. The button guide and the tool wheel stand at the screen's edges; the menus and the bag keep their 16:9 places in the middle 2240 - 320 pixels; the main menu's device, which runs off the right edge at 16:9, shows its hidden right end at 2.37. Without the setting the picture and the log are those of the unpatched build.

Played (the user, Mac app on an M4 Mac mini, a 2560x1080 screen, fullscreen): more than 5 hours at 2.37, through the end of the first region. The problems that play found (black bars and 16:9 fades on the title and loading screens, sheared title photos, squeezed text and uneven button-bar pills, spread map icons, the door wipe stopping 1920 pixels in) are the fixes above. Not checked yet (open): the regions after the first, the listed HUD panes no run has shown (the right-side 540x56 notices, the left 512x512 and 494x356 panes), photo mode (its capture at `main+0x100f12c` reads back 1920x1080), movies, the Joy-Con mouse range (`nn::hid::InitializeNmouse` with 1920x1080 at `main+0x1035b14`), the 3440x1440 and 32:9 shapes, the iPhone and iPad (the launcher offers the wide shapes on the Mac only), and v655360 (Ver. 2.0.1), whose main these stubs do not touch. The table's 2560x1440 and 3840x2160 entries suggest the game has higher render sizes of its own (not tried).

## Driving it on the Mac

The first boot shows the language list: `A`, `LUp`, `A` picks English. The character choice takes `LLeft` before `A`. From a warm cache, `A A LLeft A A Plus` reaches walking in about 300 s. On later boots, `A` at the title screen a few times continues the save and reaches walking in about 110 s. The work folder has `run/boot.sh`, `run/hdrive.sh` (no window, timed control lines) and `run/spam.sh`. The packed app takes about a minute to reach the language list (14 s of it the prepare step on a second start), so the fixed times in `run/spam.sh` miss it: press the keys once the list shows.

## Open

- The picture has not been compared with a Switch 2. The user's playtest found it fine. Sunlit ground matches published iPhone shots of the game. Shaded ground and walls are lavender. The player carries fine contour lines, already present in colour target 5435, which may be the material. In some moving shots far blocks showed through near ones; this has not been rechecked since the fixes below.
- Only the first area has been played on this build. A new save written through the game has not been checked here: the 31-minute playtest wrote none. On v327680 (a user's Mac app, November 10 to 13) more than 5 hours were played through the end of the first region, continuing the same save across days.
- The pipeline archive saved nothing on the walk from the language list to the first area, on the partial library: archive lookups found 0 and found none 655 times, and 216 bases were compiled, though the host's probe found the archive's bases. The draws' pairs seem not to be those of `shader_pairs.tsv`, which come from BNSH variations. Whether a full library differs is not checked.
- An IPA has not been built. A full iOS shader build would be needed first.
